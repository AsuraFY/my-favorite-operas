# Eugene Onegin — Independent English translation draft
## Act I · Tableau 2 · Musical numbers 8–10

**Status:** first-pass translation from the historical Russian libretto, subject to full editorial collation; not deployed. **Source:** [Wikisource/FEB Russian Act I](https://ru.wikisource.org/wiki/Евгений_Онегин_(опера)/Действие_первое), tableau 2. This is an original reading-English rendering for the project, not a rewording of Opera Guide English.

### Tableau 2 — Tatyana's room

*Late in the evening.*

### No. 8 — Entr'acte and scene with the nurse

**FILIPYEVNA**

Well, how I've chattered on!
It's late now, Tanya.
I'll have to wake you early for church;
you should go to sleep.

**TATYANA**

I cannot sleep, nurse. It's so stifling here!
Open the window, and come and sit beside me.

**FILIPYEVNA**

What is it, Tanya? What is wrong?

**TATYANA**

I'm restless. Let's talk about the old days.

**FILIPYEVNA**

What shall I tell you, Tanya?
There was a time when I remembered
so many old tales, true and invented,
of wicked spirits and young maidens.
But now my memory has grown dim;
everything I knew has slipped away.
Ah, yes! An evil time has come.
My mind has failed me.

**TATYANA**

Tell me, nurse, about your young days.
Were you in love back then?

**FILIPYEVNA**

Good heavens, Tanya! In our day
we never even heard such talk of love!
My late mother-in-law
would have driven me to my grave!

**TATYANA**

But how did you come to marry, nurse?

**FILIPYEVNA**

It must have been God's will!
My Vanya was younger than I,
my darling, and I was only thirteen.
For two weeks a matchmaker
came to visit my family,
and in the end my father blessed the marriage.
I wept bitterly with fear;
they unbraided my hair as I cried,
and led me singing into church.
And so they brought me into a stranger's family ...
But you are not listening to me!

**TATYANA**

Oh, nurse, nurse, I am suffering!
Such longing is tearing at me;
I feel so wretched, my dear.
I could burst into tears and sob my heart out!

**FILIPYEVNA**

My child, you must be ill!
May the Lord have mercy and save you!
Let me sprinkle you with holy water.
You are burning up!

**TATYANA**

I am not ill.
You see, nurse, I ... I am in love.
Leave me alone, please leave me.
I am in love.

**FILIPYEVNA**

But how can—

**TATYANA**

Go now, leave me to myself.
Bring me a pen, some paper,
and move the table nearer.
I shall go to bed soon.
Good night.

**FILIPYEVNA**

Good night, Tanya.

*She goes out.*

### No. 9 — Letter scene

**TATYANA**

Let me perish, if perish I must.
But first, in dazzling hope,
I call to a happiness I scarcely understand;
I shall taste the sweetness of being alive.
I drink the magical poison of desire;
dreams follow wherever I turn!
Everywhere—everywhere before me
stands the man who will decide my fate,
my irresistible temptation!
Everywhere he stands before me!

*She begins writing quickly, then at once tears up what she has written.*

No, no, that is not it. I'll begin again.
What is happening to me? I am aflame!
I cannot even find the words to begin.

*She thinks for a moment, then resumes writing.*

I am writing to you—what more can I say?
What else is left for me to tell you?
Now I know you have the power
to punish me with your contempt.
Yet if you feel the smallest pity
for my unhappy lot,
you will not turn away from me.

At first I meant to say nothing.
Believe me, you would never,
never have known my shame.
Never!

*She stops to think.*

Yes, I swore to hide deep in my heart
the confession of this wild, consuming love.
Alas! I can no longer command my soul!
Whatever must befall me, let it come!
I shall confess everything to him.
Courage—he must know!

*She continues writing.*

Why, oh why did you come to visit us?
If you had never come to our forgotten village,
I should not have known you;
I should not have known this bitter torment.
In time I might have mastered
the passions of my inexperienced soul.
Who knows? I might have found a friend
who understood my heart,
become a faithful wife
and a devoted mother.

Another man? No—never!
There is no one in the world
to whom I could have given my heart.
This was decided in heaven;
it is heaven's will: I am yours!
My entire life was a pledge
that I would one day meet you.
I know that God sent you to me:
until the grave you are my guardian.

You appeared to me in dreams.
Though unseen, you were already dear to me;
your wonderful gaze disturbed my heart,
your voice rang within my soul.
It was long ago—no, it was not merely a dream!
The moment you came in, I knew you.
I was overcome, my whole body burning,
and I thought: Here he is!
Here he is!

Surely I have heard you before?
You spoke to me in the stillness
when I brought aid to the poor,
or when I turned to prayer
to quiet the sorrow troubling my soul.
And at that very moment,
was it not you, beloved vision,
who appeared in the half-light
and gently bent beside my pillow?
Was it not you who whispered
words of hope and tender love?

Who are you?
My guardian angel,
or a treacherous tempter?
End these doubts of mine.
Perhaps it is all an empty dream,
the delusion of an untried heart,
and some entirely different fate awaits me.
But so be it!

From this hour I place my destiny in your hands;
I weep before you
and beg you to protect me.
I beg you!
Imagine it: I am alone here;
no one understands me.
My reason is exhausted;
I must suffer in silence until I am lost.

I wait for you!
I wait for you!
With a single word, revive
the hope within my heart;
or break this oppressive dream,
alas, with a reproach I have deserved.

I am finished. I am afraid to read it again.
I tremble with fear and shame.
Yet your honour is my safeguard;
I put my trust in it without reserve.

*The sun rises. Tatyana opens the window.*

### No. 10 — Scene and duet

**TATYANA**

Ah, the night is over.
Everything awakens; the sun is rising.
A shepherd plays his pipe; all is at peace.
But I—what has become of me?

*She falls into thought. The nurse enters.*

**FILIPYEVNA**

It's time to rise, my child, wake up!
But look, my pretty girl is dressed already.
My little early bird!
How worried I was last night.
Thank heaven you are well again;
all your nighttime sadness has vanished.
Your cheeks are red as poppies!

**TATYANA**

Ah, nurse, will you do something for me?

**FILIPYEVNA**

Certainly, my darling. Just tell me.

**TATYANA**

Please don't suspect anything ...
but you see ... oh, don't refuse me!

**FILIPYEVNA**

My dear, God is my witness!

**TATYANA**

Then send your grandson quietly
with this note to O— to that ...
that neighbour, and tell him
not to say a word to anyone,
not to mention my name.

**FILIPYEVNA**

To whom, dear heart?
My head has grown so muddled lately.
There are so many neighbours round about
that I could never count them all.
Tell me plainly, whom do you mean?

**TATYANA**

Oh nurse, how slow you are to understand!

**FILIPYEVNA**

My dearest girl, I am old now.
My wits are growing dull, Tanya.
But once I used to be sharp enough.
In the old days, when my mistress spoke a word—

**TATYANA**

Oh, nurse, this is no time for that!
Why should I care how sharp your wits are?
Can't you see this is about a letter?

**FILIPYEVNA**

Yes, yes, the letter!
Don't be cross with me, my child;
you know I have trouble understanding—

**TATYANA**

To Onegin!

**FILIPYEVNA**

Oh, yes. Now I understand.

**TATYANA**

To Onegin!
Send your grandson with the letter,
nurse—to Onegin!

**FILIPYEVNA**

There now, don't be angry, sweetheart.
You know how confused I have become.

*Filipyevna takes the letter. Tatyana turns pale.*

**FILIPYEVNA**

Why have you gone pale again?

**TATYANA**

It's nothing, nurse; really, nothing!
Please send your grandson now.

*The nurse leaves. Tatyana sits at the table and sinks back into thought.*
