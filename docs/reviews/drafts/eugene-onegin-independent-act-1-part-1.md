### Tableau 1 — The Larins' estate

*The house and garden of the Larin family. Evening is falling. Madame Larina and the nurse are making preserves. From within the house come the voices of Tatyana and Olga.*

### No. 1 — Duet and quartet

**TATYANA AND OLGA (offstage)**

Have you heard, beyond the grove at night,
the voice of one who sang of love, who sang of sorrow?
When the fields were silent in the early morning,
have you heard the sound of a shepherd's pipe,
so plaintive and so plain?
Have you heard it?

**LARINA (to the nurse)**

They are singing. And I, too, once sang,
in those long-departed years.
Do you remember? I used to sing as well.

**FILIPYEVNA**

You were young in those days!

**TATYANA AND OLGA (offstage)**

Have you sighed as you listened to the quiet voice
of one who sang of love, who sang of sorrow?
When you saw a young man among the trees
and met the gaze of his dimmed eyes,
did you sigh? Did you sigh?

**LARINA**

How fond I was of Richardson!

**FILIPYEVNA**

You were young in those days.

**LARINA**

Not that I had ever read his books;
but in those days Princess Alina,
my cousin who lived in Moscow,
was forever talking about him.

**FILIPYEVNA**

Yes, I remember, I remember.

**LARINA**

Ah, Grandison! Ah, Grandison!

**FILIPYEVNA**

Yes, I remember, I remember.
Your husband was courting you then;
but your heart was fixed upon another man,
someone you liked much better
for his mind and for his heart.

**LARINA**

Such a fashionable young gentleman he was,
a gambler and a sergeant in the Guards!

**FILIPYEVNA**

Those days are far behind us!

**LARINA**

And how elegantly I always dressed!

**FILIPYEVNA**

Always in the latest fashion!

**LARINA**

Always fashionable, always becoming!

**FILIPYEVNA**

Always fashionable, always becoming!

**LARINA**

But then, without so much as asking me—

**FILIPYEVNA**

You were suddenly carried off to be married!
And afterwards, to ease your sorrow—

**LARINA**

How bitterly I wept at first!
I almost parted from my husband!

**FILIPYEVNA**

Your husband soon brought you here.
You took charge of the household,
grew accustomed to it all,
and finally became content.

**LARINA**

I took charge of the household,
grew accustomed to it all,
and finally became content.

**FILIPYEVNA**

Thank heavens for that!

**LARINA AND FILIPYEVNA**

Habit is given to us from above;
it takes the place of happiness.
Yes, indeed, that is how it is!
Habit is given to us from above;
it takes the place of happiness.

**LARINA**

The corset, the album, Princess Polina,
the notebook of sentimental verses—
I forgot them all.

**FILIPYEVNA**

You began to call Selina by the name Akulka,
and finally exchanged—

**LARINA**

Ah!

**LARINA AND FILIPYEVNA**

The fashionable gown for a padded housecoat and a cap!
Habit is given to us from above;
it takes the place of happiness.
Yes, indeed, that is how it is!
Habit is given to us from above;
it takes the place of happiness.

**LARINA**

But my husband loved me dearly.

**FILIPYEVNA**

Yes, the master loved you dearly.

**LARINA**

He trusted me in everything, without a worry.

**FILIPYEVNA**

He trusted you in everything, without a worry.

**LARINA AND FILIPYEVNA**

Habit is given to us from above;
it takes the place of happiness.

### No. 2 — Peasants' chorus and dance

*Peasants are heard singing in the distance.*

**SOLO SINGER (offstage)**

My nimble feet are aching
from all their walking.

**PEASANTS (offstage)**

Those nimble feet from all their walking!

**SOLO SINGER**

My fair hands are aching
from all their labour.

**PEASANTS**

Those fair hands from all their labour!
My ardent heart aches
with care and worry.
I do not know what to do
or how to forget my beloved!
My nimble feet are aching
from all their walking.
My fair hands are aching
from all their labour,
my fair hands from all their labour.

*The peasants enter, carrying a decorated sheaf of grain.*

**PEASANTS**

Greetings, dear lady, our mistress!
Greetings, our generous provider!
Here we have come before you;
we have brought a decorated sheaf.
Our harvest work is done!

**LARINA**

Well, splendid! Enjoy yourselves.
I am happy for you. Sing us something livelier!

**PEASANTS**

Gladly, mistress; we shall entertain you!
Come, girls, gather in a ring!
Come along; take your places!

*The young peasants form a circle around the sheaf and dance. The others sing. Tatyana, holding a book, and Olga appear on the balcony.*

**PEASANTS**

Along the little bridge,
over the boards of guelder rose—
Vainu, vainu, vainu, vainu—
over the boards of guelder rose!

There passed a handsome young fellow,
rosy as a raspberry—
Vainu, vainu, vainu, vainu—
rosy as a raspberry!

He carried a club upon his shoulder,
and beneath his coat a bagpipe—
Vainu, vainu, vainu, vainu—
beneath his coat a bagpipe!
Under the other side, a little fiddle.
Guess it, dearest friend—
Vainu, vainu, vainu, vainu—
guess it, dearest friend!

The sun has set. Are you not sleeping?
Either come outside or send someone out—
Vainu, vainu, vainu, vainu—
either come outside or send someone out!
Send Sasha, or send Masha,
or dear Parasha—
Vainu, vainu, vainu, vainu—
or dear Parasha!

Send Sasha, or send Masha,
or dear Parasha!
Little Parasha came outdoors
and spoke with her sweetheart—
Vainu, vainu, vainu, vainu—
and spoke with her sweetheart:

“Do not judge me, dearest friend;
I came out dressed just as I was,
in my worn little shift,
in my short little underskirt—
Vainu, vainu, vainu, vainu—
in my worn little shift,
in my short little underskirt!”
Vainu!


### No. 3 — Scene and Olga’s aria

**TATYANA**

When I hear these songs,
how I love to let my thoughts drift away,
far away into a world of dreams!

**OLGA**

Oh, Tanya, Tanya!
You are always dreaming.
I am so unlike you;
a song always puts me in a cheerful mood.

*She dances a few steps.*

“Along the little bridge,
over the boards of guelder rose ...”

I am not made for languishing in sorrow;
I do not care to dream in solitude,
or sigh on a balcony
in the darkness of night,
sigh and sigh from the depths of my heart.
Why should I sigh when my young days
are passing so happily?
I am lighthearted and mischievous;
everyone still calls me a child.
Life will always be dear to me;
and I shall remain as I have always been,
like carefree, shifting hope itself:
playful, heedless, joyful.

I am not made for languishing in sorrow;
I do not care to dream in solitude,
or sigh on a balcony
in the darkness of night,
sigh and sigh from the depths of my heart.
Why should I sigh when my young days
are passing so happily?
I am lighthearted and mischievous;
everyone still calls me a child.

### No. 4 — Scene

**LARINA**

There you are, you little scamp,
my bright and lively bird!
I suppose you are ready
to start dancing right now, aren't you?

**FILIPYEVNA (to Tatyana)**

Tanyusha! Ah, Tanyusha, what is wrong?
Are you ill?

**TATYANA**

No, nurse, I am well.

**LARINA (to the peasants)**

Thank you, my dears, for your songs.
Go over to the servants’ wing.
*To Filipyevna:* And see that they are given wine.
Farewell, my friends!

**PEASANTS**

Farewell, dear mistress!

*The peasants leave; the nurse follows. Tatyana sits on the terrace steps, absorbed in her book.*

**OLGA**

Mamma, just look at Tanya!

**LARINA**

What about her? *Looking more closely:* Why, my dear,
you are terribly pale.

**TATYANA**

That is how I always look; please do not worry, Mamma.
What I am reading is so interesting!

**LARINA (laughing)**

So that is why you are pale?

**TATYANA**

Of course, Mamma! The story of two lovers
and all their heartache moves me so deeply.
I feel so sorry for them.
How terribly they suffer, both of them!

**LARINA**

Enough, Tanya.
I used to be just like you;
such books stirred me once, too.
But all of it is make-believe.
The years passed, and I learned
that life has no such heroes.
Now I am quite at peace.

**OLGA**

A little too much at peace!
Look—you forgot to take off your apron.
What will you do when Lensky arrives?

*Larina hurriedly removes her apron as Olga laughs. Wheels approach and sleigh bells are heard.*

**OLGA**

Listen! Someone is arriving. It must be him!

**LARINA**

So it is!

**TATYANA (looking down from the terrace)**

He is not alone.

**LARINA**

Who can it be?

**FILIPYEVNA (hurrying in with a young servant)**

Madam, Mr Lensky has arrived;
Mr Onegin is with him!

**TATYANA**

Oh! I must run away!

**LARINA (stopping her)**

Where are you going, Tanya?
People will think ill of you!
Good heavens, my cap is crooked!

**OLGA (to Larina)**

Tell them to come in!

**LARINA (to the servant)**

Go, quickly! Ask them in at once!

*The servant hurries away. Everyone prepares in great agitation to receive the visitors. Filipyevna straightens Tatyana’s clothes, gestures reassuringly to her, and withdraws.*

### No. 5 — Scene and quartet

*Lensky and Onegin enter. Lensky kisses Larina's hand and bows respectfully to the two daughters.*

**LENSKY**

Ladies, I have taken the liberty
of bringing a friend with me.
May I present Onegin, my neighbour?

**ONEGIN (bowing)**

I am delighted!

**LARINA (flustered)**

Oh, not at all! We are delighted to see you.
Do sit down. These are my daughters.

**ONEGIN**

I am very, very pleased!

**LARINA**

Shall we go inside?
Or would you rather remain in the open air?
Please, no ceremony.
We are neighbours; there is no need
to stand on formalities!

**LENSKY**

How charming it is here!
I love this quiet, shaded garden.
It feels so welcoming.

**LARINA (to her daughters)**

Excellent! I shall go and see to the house.
Please entertain our guests. I shall be back.

*Onegin draws near Lensky and speaks quietly. Tatyana and Olga stand apart, lost in thought.*

**ONEGIN (to Lensky)**

Tell me, which one is Tatyana?

**LENSKY**

The one who is sad and silent,
just like Svetlana.

**ONEGIN**

How curious!
Are you really in love with the younger sister?

**LENSKY**

Why do you ask?

**ONEGIN**

Were I a poet like you,
I would have chosen the other one.

**TATYANA (aside)**

At last! My eyes have opened!
I know it—I know that he is the one!

**OLGA**

Ah, I knew that Onegin's arrival
would make a great impression on everyone,
giving all the neighbours something to talk about.
There will be guess after guess ...

**LENSKY**

Ah, my dear friend ...

**ONEGIN**

There is no life in Olga’s features,
just like the Madonna in a Van Dyck painting.
Her face is round and rosy,
like that foolish moon
in that foolish sky!

**LENSKY**

A wave and a rock,
poetry and prose, ice and flame,
are not so unlike each other
as you and I are different!

**TATYANA**

Alas, from now on day and night,
even my most feverish solitary dreams,
will bring his beloved image back to me!

**OLGA**

They will whisper among themselves,
tease us, gossip, and pass judgment!
One guess will follow another.

**TATYANA**

A mysterious power will speak
of him to me without ceasing
and set my very soul ablaze with love!

**OLGA**

They will tease and gossip and judge
and find a husband for Tanya!

**TATYANA**

Everything will speak of him to me
and set my soul ablaze with love!

### No. 6 — Scene and Lensky’s arioso

**LENSKY**

How happy I am—how happy!
I see you again!

**OLGA**

But surely we saw each other yesterday.

**LENSKY**

Yes, of course!
And yet a whole long day
has passed while we were apart.
It seemed an eternity!

**OLGA**

An eternity!
What a dreadful word!

**LENSKY**

Perhaps ...
but it does not frighten my love.

*Lensky and Olga walk deeper into the garden.*

**ONEGIN (to Tatyana)**

Tell me, don't you find life
dreadfully tedious in this remote place,
charming though it is?
I cannot imagine you have
very many diversions.

**TATYANA**

I read a great deal.

**ONEGIN**

Indeed. Reading provides endless nourishment
for the mind and heart.
But one cannot always sit with a book!

**TATYANA**

Sometimes I wander through the garden
and lose myself in dreams.

**ONEGIN**

What do you dream about?

**TATYANA**

Thoughtfulness has been my companion
ever since I lay in the cradle.

**ONEGIN**

I see that you are very much a dreamer.
I was like that once myself.

*Onegin and Tatyana continue their conversation as they walk away along a garden path. Lensky and Olga return.*

**LENSKY**

I love you;
I love you, Olga,
with the passion that only
a poet's restless soul can feel.
One dream, one longing, one sorrow—
always the same, wherever I go!
You captivated me when I was still a boy,
before I knew the pain of love.
Tenderly I watched your childhood games.
In the shelter of the grove
I shared those games with you.
I love you, I love you,
as only the soul of a poet can love.
You alone live in my dreams;
you are my only desire,
my joy and my suffering.
I love you, I love you.
And nothing, nothing—
not the chill of distance,
not an hour of separation,
not the noise of celebration—
can sober a soul warmed
by the first pure flame of love!

**OLGA**

Beneath the shelter of our quiet country home
you and I grew up together.

**LENSKY**

I love you!

**OLGA**

And do you remember how our fathers
planned to marry us
when we were still children?

**LENSKY**

I love you, I love you!

### No. 7 — Closing scene

**LARINA**

Ah, there you are! Where has Tanya gone?

**FILIPYEVNA**

She must be walking by the pond with our guest.
I shall go and call her.

**LARINA**

And tell her that it is time to go inside
and offer our hungry guests whatever
the house can provide.
*To Lensky:* Do come in, please.

**LENSKY**

We will follow you.

*Larina enters the house. Olga and Lensky follow a little way behind. Tatyana and Onegin come slowly towards the house from the pond, with the nurse walking at a distance.*

**ONEGIN (to Tatyana)**

My uncle was a man of the strictest honour.
When he became seriously ill
he made everyone show him proper respect;
he could not have devised a better plan.
Let others learn from his example!

*They have reached the terrace.*

But heavens, what terrible boredom
to sit with a sick man day and night,
never leaving him for an instant!

*Onegin and Tatyana enter the house.*

**FILIPYEVNA (aside)**

My little dove walks quietly along,
her head bent, her eyes cast down,
so shy and modest!
Well, now—
could she have taken a fancy
to this new gentleman?

*The nurse walks into the house, shaking her head thoughtfully.*
