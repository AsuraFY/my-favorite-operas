# Eugene Onegin — Independent English translation draft
## Act II · Tableau 5 · Nos. 17–18

**Status:** first-pass independently prepared English from [Russian Act II, tableau 5 (Wikisource/FEB)](https://ru.wikisource.org/wiki/Евгений_Онегин_(опера)/Действие_второе); not yet editorially accepted or deployed.

### Tableau 5 — The old mill

*An abandoned mill, the appointed place for the duel. Early on a winter morning, Lensky and his second Zaretsky await Onegin.*

### No. 17 — Introduction, scene and Lensky's aria

**ZARETSKY**

Well, then?
It seems our opponent hasn't arrived.

**LENSKY**

He will be here presently.

**ZARETSKY**

Still, I find it rather strange
that he isn't here already.
It's nearly seven o'clock!
I thought he would be waiting for us.

*Zaretsky walks towards the mill. Lensky sits lost in thought.*

**LENSKY**

Where, where have you gone,
golden days of my springtime?
What does tomorrow hold for me?
My gaze tries vainly to discover it;
it is hidden in the deepest darkness.
It matters little:
the law of fate is just.

Whether I fall, pierced by the bullet,
or whether the bullet passes me by,
all is for the best;
the hour allotted for waking and for sleep
comes to each of us.
Blessed be the day with all its cares,
and blessed the coming of the night.

Tomorrow morning's first ray may shine;
bright day will begin again,
while I, perhaps, shall have descended
to the secret shadows of the grave.
And slow-flowing Lethe will swallow
the memory of a youthful poet.
The world will forget me.

But you—you, Olga—
tell me, lovely maiden,
will you come to shed a tear
over my early grave?
Will you remember:
“He loved me;
to me alone he dedicated
the sad dawn of his storm-tossed life”?

Ah, Olga, how I loved you!
To you alone I dedicated
the sad dawn of my storm-tossed life.
Ah, Olga, how I loved you!

Dearest friend, beloved friend,
come, come to me!
Beloved friend, come; I am your bridegroom.
Come; I am your bridegroom!
Come, come!
I await you, my beloved.
Come, come; I am your bridegroom.

Where, where have you gone,
golden days, golden days of my spring?

*Onegin appears with his valet Guillot. Zaretsky sees them and approaches Lensky.*

### No. 18 — The duel

**ZARETSKY**

Ah, here they are!
But who is that with your friend?
I cannot make him out.

**ONEGIN (bowing)**

Forgive me for being late.
I have kept you waiting a little.

**ZARETSKY**

One moment! Where is your second?
I am a classicist, a pedant, in matters of duelling;
I value proper methods so highly
that when I lay a man out flat
I insist upon doing it according
to all the rules of the art,
in keeping with ancient custom.

**ONEGIN**

A principle for which
you deserve every compliment!
My second? Here he is:
Monsieur Guillot!
I do not foresee any objection
to my introduction.
He may not be an important person,
but he is certainly an honest fellow.
Shall we begin?

**LENSKY**

We may as well begin!

*Zaretsky and Guillot prepare the weapons and the ground. Lensky and Onegin stand silently, each lost in thought.*

**LENSKY AND ONEGIN (each aside)**

Enemies!
Was it long ago that thirst for blood
drove us apart?
Was it so long ago that we shared
our free hours, our meals,
our thoughts, our undertakings as friends?
And now, in hatred,
like hereditary foes,
we prepare in silence
to bring about each other's death
with cold deliberation.

Ah! Should we not laugh instead,
before blood has stained our hands,
and part from one another as friends?
No! No! No! No!

*Zaretsky places the adversaries apart and hands them their pistols. Guillot hides behind a tree.*

**ZARETSKY**

Now approach each other!

*Zaretsky claps his hands three times. The opponents advance four paces each and take aim. Onegin fires first. Lensky falls. Onegin and Zaretsky rush towards him.*

**ONEGIN**

Is he dead?

**ZARETSKY**

Dead!

*Onegin seizes his head in horror.*
